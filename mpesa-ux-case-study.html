01 — Problem Statement

Kenyans move money, but don't manage it

M-Pesa processes billions of shillings daily across Kenya. For most users, it has become their primary bank — used for rent, groceries, salaries, savings, and everything in between. Yet the platform offers no meaningful view of where that money goes.

Millions of Kenyans use M-Pesa as their primary financial tool but lack structured visibility into their own spending patterns. There is no budgeting feature, no spending categorisation, and no forward-looking financial guidance — leaving users unable to make informed decisions with their money even when it is all flowing through a single platform.

03 — Pain Points

What's breaking down in their financial lives

Across all four personas, eight core pain points emerged. These were validated against the diary study data and ranked by frequency and severity of impact on financial wellbeing.

🔴
Zero spending visibility
Users receive SMS per transaction but have no cumulative view. They cannot see what they spent on food last week vs this week without manually counting SMSes.
🟠
Month-end shock
Most users only realise they've overspent when they're unable to pay rent or send money home. There is no early warning system in the current M-Pesa experience.
🔴
Savings without structure
Users want to save but set aside money without a goal or timeline. Funds earmarked for savings are frequently repurposed within days due to lack of commitment mechanisms.
🟠
Business and personal funds mixed
SME users like James cannot separate personal transactions from business ones. This makes it impossible to understand actual household expenditure or business profitability.
🔵
Low financial literacy in context
Generic budgeting advice doesn't resonate. Users need guidance tied to their own transactions — not abstract rules about "saving 20% of income" when income itself is irregular.
🟢
No shared household budgeting
Many households split financial responsibilities between spouses or family members transacting via separate M-Pesa lines. There is no mechanism to plan or track as a household unit.
🔵
Chama transparency
Chama (savings group) treasurers manage contributions manually. Members cannot verify their contribution history, and disputes arise frequently over who paid what and when.
🟠
Variable income, fixed needs
Freelancers and informal workers like Amara earn inconsistent amounts but face consistent rent, school fees, and utility bills. No tool helps them model this volatility proactively.

05 — Solution

A budgeting assistant that knows your money

The M-Pesa Smart Budgeting Assistant is a native AI layer built directly into the M-Pesa app. It uses real transaction data — already flowing through the platform — to automatically categorise spending, surface insights, and offer proactive guidance in plain Swahili and English.

Unlike generic budgeting apps that require manual data entry, this system works with what users already do. The intelligence is contextual: it speaks to Wanjiku about her specific spending, not budgeting theory.

Automatic transaction categorisation
Every M-Pesa transaction is tagged in real time — food, transport, utilities, school fees, chama, savings — using merchant data and recipient patterns. No user input required.
Conversational AI budget coach
Users can ask questions in natural language — "Nimetumia pesa ngapi kwa chakula this week?" — and receive instant, personalised answers based on their own data.
Proactive nudges before the problem
Smart alerts fire when a user is on track to overspend a category, when salary arrives and a budget hasn't been set, or when a savings goal deadline is approaching.
Salary-cycle budget planner
On payday, the assistant surfaces a one-tap budget template based on last month's spending. Users can adjust targets per category and lock a savings amount before it can be spent.
Business mode for SME users
James can tag any number as "personal" or "business" with one tap. The assistant maintains separate summaries and can generate a simple end-of-month business report.

06 — Feature Breakdown

Six features that change the relationship with money

Each feature was validated against at least two personas and mapped to a specific pain point from the research. No feature was included that didn't have a clear user need behind it.

01
Auto-Categorisation Engine
Every M-Pesa transaction is automatically tagged using a combination of till number data, paybill codes, and recipient phone number patterns (e.g. a recurring number each Friday = family remittance). Categories include Food, Transport, Utilities, School Fees, Healthcare, Chama/Savings, and Business. Users can override or create custom categories with one tap.
Addresses: Zero visibility pain point
02
Payday Budget Planner
When M-Pesa detects an incoming salary or large deposit, it surfaces a guided budget-setting flow. Pre-filled categories are drawn from the previous month's spending. Users can set limits per category, allocate a savings amount (locked via M-Pesa Lock Savings integration), and confirm in under 60 seconds. Available in Swahili and English.
Addresses: Month-end shock, savings without structure
03
Conversational AI Budget Coach
A natural language interface embedded in the M-Pesa app. Users can ask questions like "Nimetumia pesa ngapi kwa chakula?" (How much have I spent on food?) or "How much can I spend today and still make rent?" The assistant answers using live transaction data and adds contextual advice — for example, flagging that this week's spending is 20% above the same week last month.
Addresses: Low financial literacy, no visibility
04
Smart Spending Nudges
Proactive push notifications fire at the right moment, not randomly. Triggers include: reaching 75% of a category budget, a large unusual transaction outside typical behaviour, a savings goal deadline within 7 days, or a good moment (mid-month surplus) to accelerate saving. Nudge frequency is adaptive — users who ignore nudges see fewer; engaged users see more detailed ones.
Addresses: Month-end shock, impulse spending
05
Business / Personal Split Mode
SME users can activate Business Mode, which adds a "tag this transaction" option to every M-Pesa payment and receipt. Over time the system learns common business recipients (e.g. supplier paybills) and auto-tags them. A separate Business Dashboard shows monthly revenue, expenses, and a simple profit summary — exportable as a PDF for SACCO loan applications or KRA filings.
Addresses: Business and personal funds mixed
06
Household & Chama Shared View
Users can invite a spouse or household co-manager to a shared budget view (read-only or collaborative). Each person's transactions flow into a combined household dashboard. For chama groups, a Treasurer Mode allows contribution tracking, automated reminder sends before meeting day, and a shared group ledger all members can verify — reducing disputes and manual record-keeping.
Addresses: Shared household budgeting, chama transparency
